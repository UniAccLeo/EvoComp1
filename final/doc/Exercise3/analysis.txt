The EA that I created used uniform crossover to generate the children, and occasionally used the same formula as 1+1 for
generating random mutations.

Because this algorithm runs with a population pool rather than an individual, it takes more iterations for overall improvement since
the population must all be updated. This was observed most evidently in problems 1 and 3, where my EA was still able to find the optimal
solution but slightly slower than 1+1 and rls. Interestingly my algorithm was able to perform better in problem2 which counts leading ones,
suggestion that perhaps it is better at finding local patterns than the other two algorithms. The positives of this evolutionary algorithm
is also easy to see, which is that using a population allows for more variations and patterns to be tested, helping to prevent local peaks
from blocking exploration. This was more pronounced in problems 23 and more evidently in 24, where despite still reaching a plateau, my EA
managed to get slightly higher results than the other algorithms. The wider deviation also shows that my algorithm is more likely to explore
different optinos, explaining why it performs a bit better in these tasks where exploration is needed.
