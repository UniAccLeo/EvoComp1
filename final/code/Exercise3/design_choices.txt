Exercise 3 – Design choices

Scope
We target MaxCoverage and MaxInfluence instances (IDs 2100–2103, 2200–2203) with a uniform constraint using two evolutionary algorithms:
- population-based single-objective EA (SOEA) with repair and diversity mechanisms. 
- population-based multi-objective EA (MOEA) based on NSGA‑II acting on (value, -cost) objectives.
Each algorithm is evaluated for population sizes 10, 20, 50, with 30 independent runs and a budget of 10,000 fitness evaluations per run.

Single-Objective EA (SOEA)
Representation. Bitstring over items/nodes.  
Fitness. IOH's submodular objective; negative values indicate infeasibility (cost limit violated). Infeasible offspring are repaired until feasible.

Repair operator 
We use marginal-drop repair only: repeatedly remove the 1‑bit with the smallest marginal contribution (re-evaluating after flipping it to 0) until the solution becomes feasible. This respects the submodular structure and preserves high-value elements.  
Random-drop repair was tested initially for comparison but omitted from the final experiments after observing weaker performance.

Variation. Bit-flip mutation with rate 1/n.  
Diversity. Deterministic crowding (offspring competes with the more similar parent by Hamming distance) plus a mild fitness-sharing step that replaces the most crowded individual with a random immigrant when not worse.  
Selection/Survival. Pairwise deterministic crowding plus diversity replacement ensures exploration and prevents premature convergence.
Rationale. Submodular objectives reward good marginal gains; marginal-drop repair directly uses this insight to maintain feasibility with minimal value loss. It also provides a consistent baseline for fair comparison across population sizes and problem types.

Multi‑Objective EA (MOEA)
We optimise (value, −cost) using a simplified NSGA‑II pipeline:
- Fast non‑dominated sorting for rank assignment.
- Crowding distance for within-front diversity.
- Tournament selection by (rank, crowding).
- Bit‑flip mutation only (no crossover).
- Environmental selection to maintain the target population size.

This MOEA complements Exercise 2’s GSEMO by exploring the Pareto front with a larger population and stronger diversity pressure.

Budget Accounting
All fitness calls go through the IOH problem(x) oracle so the evaluation counter inside IOH controls the total budget.  
Repairs that compute marginal contributions intentionally consume additional evaluations, trading computational cost for better-quality feasible solutions.

Generative AI Use & Process Disclosure
We used a generative AI to brainstorm design variants and to draft boilerplate for runner and plotting scripts.
All algorithmic logic, parameters, and IOH integration were implemented, tested, and verified by us.
