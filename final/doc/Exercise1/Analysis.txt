From the plots produced for the single objective approaches, there are many conclusion we can draw about the algorithms and the
problems themselves. For starters, it is important to note that all three evolutionary algorithms outperformed random search,
showing how the mutations are indeed leading to a better solution. Furthermore, most often the 1+1 EA is able to produce the 
best results, most likely due to its ability to make larger mutations sometimes which allows for more exploration. This is then 
followed by my EA and then rls. Looking past the final results though, it can be seen that usually rls actually picks up a lead 
earlier on, but is then caught up by the other algorithms as it reaches a plateau - likely as the result of reaching a local optima.
This explains why in questions 22XX, where there was no plateau for rls, it managed to outperform both 1+1 EA and myEA convincingly.
In terms of the problems themselves, this suggests that the 22XX problems value small changes, perhaps more often than not the value
of a bit is not dependent on other bits (so a single flipped bit correctly causes an improvement), whereas the other problems have more
interactions between bits, where only by flipping two or more bits simultaneously can it break past a local optima.
