This is an analysis of ACO algorithm compared against MMAS and MMAS* algorithms. 
The MMAS variants significantly outperformed the basic ACO program across nearly all programs.

On problem 1 (OneMax), MMAS and MMAS* converged around 100, reaching the global maxima, 
while ACO only achieves slightly under 80. ACO shows slower, linear improvement throughout 
the function evaluations while the MMAS variants climb steeply and plateau at a global maximum.

For Problem 2 (LeadingOnes), ACO initially performs better, however converges prematurely 
and plateaus. Meanwhile, MMAS and MMAS* display exponential improvement with the function 
evaluations, finally plateauing at a higher point than ACO.

In Problem 3, MMAS and MMAS* follow a similar smooth improvement, plateauing at a maximum 
around 5000. While ACO shows irregular progress with visible random jumps, it eventually 
catches up near the end of its budget, reaching approximately the same maximum as MMAS 
and MMAS*. These random jumps suggest ACO's less structured search process where it makes 
sudden discoveries.

Problems 23 and 24 illustrate similar results with MMAS and MMAS* having smooth improvement 
throughout the function evaluations compared to irregular and random jumps with the ACO program. 
However, with these problems the ACO program eventually plateaued to similar maximums as the MMAS variants.

Problems 18 and 25 illustrate a key drawback of the ACO program. ACO prematurely converged upon 
a local maximum in these problems compared to the MMAS variants which outperformed it significantly.

In the growth phase across problems, MMAS and MMAS* increase steadily and at similar rates, while 
ACO demonstrates much slower and more inconsistent progress. The MMAS variants climb steeply through 
the middle regions and converge efficiently to high-quality solutions.
