Problem 1:
RLS and 1+1 were both able to find the optimal solution at 100 early on.
whereas random search could not. RLS was able to do this slightly faster than 1+1.

Problem 2:
Once again, RLS and 1+1 were able to find the optimal solution, with RLS being faster, and random search
was unable to find the best solution. Interestingly here we can see that random search found better solutions at
lower iterations as the EAs were unable to observe useful patterns yet.

Problem 3:
Problem 3 presented the recurring pattern that the EAs were more competent at finding the best solution,
whereas the random solution was once again unable to do so. This time though the EAs didn't even start off 
at a disadvantage due to random starts, and this led them to perform exceptionally well against random search.
RLS is also keeping its edge over 1+1 EA.

Problem 18:
In this problem, while the EAs still performed better than the random search, 1+1 EA was able
to perform better than RLS. It can be observed that the rls algorithm reached a plateau at around 3.7,
likely due to no single bit-switch being enough to make a meaningful enough change, showing off the strength 
of the 1+1 algorithm for more potential exploration

Problem 23:
Problem 23 reverted back to the original trend of RLS performing slightly better than 1+1
and both being able to find the optimal solution over random search.

Problem 24:
Problem 24 was another one which fit the trend, and once again showing how initially random search
is able to outperform the other two algorithms through sheer luck, but is eventually overtaken

Problem 25:
To finish is off, Problem 25 showed the same trend as in 18, where despite getting an early lead,
the RLS algorithm found a local plateau and got stuck, allowing the 1+1 algorithm to overtake it
and find a more optimal solution at the end of the runs.
